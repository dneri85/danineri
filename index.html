Case studies

Problems I’ve solved.

Two examples from my research operations work: the problem, my responsibility, what I did, and the outcome.

Case 1

Research operations · Voxelmaps

Making sure the right participants reached the study

01The problem

Participants were being booked into studies without meeting the required qualifications. Each one had to be turned away, which wasted session time and slowed projects down.

02My responsibility

I oversaw participant scheduling and onboarding, so it was my job to make sure the people we booked were the people each study needed.

03What I did

I reviewed the booking process and screening documents and traced the problem to communication and documentation gaps. I updated the documents with the correct requirements and trained recruiters on what to check and who to contact with questions.

04The outcome

Rejection rates went down, recruiters and the project team worked from the same accurate information, and more sessions ran with qualified participants.

Why it matters in clinical research: clinical studies depend on the same discipline, with clear eligibility criteria, accurate documentation, and well-trained staff.

Case 2

Research operations · Tata Elxsi

Cutting delays in venue recruitment

01The problem

Recruiting venues for research sessions was slow. Each venue's status, qualification details, and next steps weren't kept in one place, so venues were rejected late and work stalled between vendors, venue owners, and our team.

02My responsibility

As project manager for UX operations, I worked with multiple venue recruitment vendors and venue owners and was responsible for getting qualified venues ready on schedule.

03What I did

I built a shared tracker showing each venue's status, qualifications, and next steps, and used it to give vendors clear requirements so venues met compliance standards before they were submitted.

04The outcome

Venue recruitment delays dropped, fewer venues were rejected, and everyone worked from the same accurate data, which closed communication gaps and kept projects moving.

Why it matters in clinical research: selecting and qualifying research sites works the same way, with clear standards, shared tracking, and steady communication with outside partners.

My story

Brazil, New York, Memphis, California, Florida.

  1. 🇧🇷

    The beginning

    Born in Brazil, wired for curiosity

    Before I ever thought about systems, workflows, or efficiency, I was just a kid obsessed with how things fit together. Legos were basically my first job. Structure, logic, the quiet satisfaction of making pieces click into place. I didn't know it then, but that instinct would follow me everywhere.

  2. 🗽

    Age 14: New York

    Everything changed overnight

    At 14, my family packed up and chased the American dream to New York. New language. New culture. New everything. It was disorienting, humbling, and, looking back, one of the best things that ever happened to me. You learn real adaptability when you have no choice but to figure it out. No manual, no fallback, just forward.

  3. 🎸

    2012: Tennessee

    Memphis, logistics, and thinking at scale

    I got married, settled in Tennessee, and landed in the middle of one of the world's great logistics hubs. Memphis moves things, millions of packages, truckloads, supply chains, all flowing through this one city. I was hooked. Watching complex operations run (or fail to run) at that scale lit something up in me: a genuine obsession with efficiency, coordination, and making systems actually work.

  4. 🌉

    2016: California

    Operations, evolved

    West Coast, new chapter. I kept building on what I'd learned, but the work got sharper. Less about moving boxes, more about improving how people and systems interact. Workflows, processes, the invisible friction that slows organizations down. If something could run smoother, I wanted to know why it wasn't, and how to fix it.

  5. 🌴

    2026: Florida

    Closer to family, clear on the mission

    Moved to Florida to be closer to family while continuing my graduate studies in health informatics. A different kind of chapter, one rooted in showing up for the people who shaped me, and in naming the work I'd already been doing for years: helping healthcare and community organizations close the digital gaps that leave people behind.

  6. Today

    Where it's all coming together

    Operations. Data. Digital equity. Three things that, when they actually work in harmony, don't just improve a process, they change who gets left out of the system. That's where my focus is now, through Solara Vita, my digital navigation practice: closing the gaps that keep older adults and underserved communities from getting the care and information they need, and building experiences that are genuinely accessible to everyone. The kid who loved Legos is still in there, just building bigger, more inclusive systems now.
